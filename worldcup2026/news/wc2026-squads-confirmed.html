The World Cup Is Finally Here.

The squads are confirmed, the dreams are set — and Messi, Ronaldo and Ochoa are doing it all one more time.

Right then. It's actually happening. After years of hype, planning and qualification drama, the FIFA World Cup 2026 squads are officially confirmed — and honestly, just looking through the 48 lists gives you that familiar feeling in your stomach. That mixture of excitement and impatience that only a World Cup can produce.

FIFA confirmed all 48 squad lists on 2 June, ahead of kick-off on Thursday 11 June. Across three host nations — the United States, Mexico and Canada — a record 1,248 players will battle across 104 matches for the most famous trophy in sport. This is the biggest World Cup ever held, and the squads reflect that.

Nearly 900 First-Timers

What really jumps out when you look at these squads is how many players are experiencing the World Cup for the first time. Of the 1,248 players named, 891 have never been to a World Cup before. Only 357 are returning with previous experience. That means roughly seven in every ten players on these planes will have never felt anything like what awaits them.

There is something genuinely moving about that. Somewhere in those 891 names is a kid who grew up watching the tournament on a small television, who dreamed about this moment their entire life — and now it's real. That's what makes the World Cup different from everything else.

"891 players are about to experience a World Cup for the first time. Every single one of them has a story that brought them here."

Messi, Ronaldo and Ochoa — One More Time

Let's be honest — the two names everyone checks for first are Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo. And yes, both are here. Both are heading into their sixth World Cup, which is a statistic that barely makes sense when you say it out loud. Six tournaments. For context, that spans from 2006 all the way to 2026 — twenty years of World Cups.

Messi arrives as the reigning world champion, having finally lifted the trophy in Qatar 2022. He's 38 now and the Argentina squad is built around protecting him while also giving him the platform to produce magic one final time. Ronaldo, now 41, remains arguably the most controversial selection in world football — some think he's past it, others think he'll prove everyone wrong. Either way, the cameras will find him.

And then there's Guillermo Ochoa. Mexico's goalkeeper, 40 years old, heading to his sixth World Cup. The man has been making impossible saves on the biggest stage since 2006. He deserves every moment of this send-off.

Four Nations Making Their World Cup Debut

This is the part that genuinely excites me about a 48-team World Cup. Cabo Verde, Curaçao, Jordan and Uzbekistan are all appearing at the tournament for the very first time. Think about what that means for those countries — the players, the fans, the families watching from home.

Uzbekistan's debut stands out in particular. Manchester City defender Abdukodir Khusanov leads a squad that has united an entire nation. These players are not just footballers right now — they are national heroes before they have even kicked a ball in a World Cup match.

The Young Stars Ready to Explode

Every World Cup produces at least one player who arrives relatively unknown and leaves as a global superstar. In 1998 it was Ronaldo. In 2006 it was Messi. In 2014 it was Neymar. Who is it this time?

France's Warren Zaïre-Emery is the name on everyone's lips. Still a teenager, already a Champions League winner with PSG, and now heading into his first World Cup with the reigning European giants behind him. Morocco's Bilal El Khannouss is another one to watch — quick, direct, unpredictable. And New Zealand's Finn Surman could be the tournament's surprise package from the Pacific.

These are the players who haven't yet had their world stage moment. In a few weeks, all of that could change.

A Truly Global Tournament

The numbers behind these squads are staggering. The 1,248 players come from 449 different clubs across 71 countries. Some nations have gone almost entirely domestic — Qatar and Saudi Arabia have 25 of their 26 players based at home clubs. Others, like Senegal, Uruguay and Côte d'Ivoire, have drawn their entire squads from leagues abroad.

There is no single dominant league anymore. The Premier League, La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A, Ligue 1 — they are all represented at the very highest level. Modern football is genuinely global, and this squad list confirms it.

The oldest player in the tournament is Scotland's Craig Gordon at 43. The youngest is Mexico's Gilberto Mora at 17. Over 25 years separate them — but on 11 June, they both become World Cup players. That's the beauty of this competition.
